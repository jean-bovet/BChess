BChess · iPhone and Mac · design review

The board is the app. Everything else earns its place.

Today the game screen shows 19 controls and readouts, several of them twice. The proposal keeps 11 on screen, puts the rest one tap away, and turns the engine’s statistics into one sentence a player can use.

Three rules for the screen

  1. Board first. The board gets the full width and nothing competes with it for attention.
  2. One way to do each thing. Undo/Redo and the ◀ ▶ buttons do the same thing today; keep one.
  3. Show what a player reads, hide what an engine author reads. “White is slightly better, best is h3” beats “Depth 12/18 with 1,204,331 nodes at 2,113,904 n/s”.

What changes

Merge
Undo / Redo and ◀ ▶

Both step back and forward through the moves (GameSession.undo() is a move backward). Keep one pair, in the bottom bar. Touch and hold for the start or the end of the game.

Move
Copy & Paste

Used rarely, yet it holds a bottom-bar slot today. It moves into the ⋯ menu with Share, which leaves the top bar with two buttons.

Merge
Share

Today a separate top-bar button. In the proposal it is “Share Game” in the ⋯ menu.

Replace
The (i) panel

The panel splits by what a player needs. The last few moves are always visible as a one-line strip: tap a move to jump to it, tap the strip for every move with comments and variations. The engine gets its own button.

Simplify
Engine readout

One sentence, the evaluation, a thin bar, and the best line, shown only while the engine button is on. Depth, nodes and nodes per second go away on iPhone (on the Mac they could stay behind a setting, see D3).

Remove
Game name row (“-”)

The PGN event name is almost always empty. The title already names the game.

Rename
Title “Game 2026-10-05 14.28.29”

The title shows the players (“You vs Computer”) and the opening underneath when it is known. The date stays in the Games list.

Simplify
Player rows

“Computer · 5 s” and “You · to move” read better than a cpu icon, a person icon, “White”, “Black” and a hollow or filled triangle. A dot shows whose turn it is. Captured pieces stay, they are small and useful.

Move
New Game, “Settings”, Flip Board

They stay in the ⋯ menu. The sheet titled “Settings” becomes “Players & Level”, which is what it edits.

Keep
Variation arrows on the board

When the next move has alternatives, the arrows on the board stay the way to pick one. The ⑂ mark in the strip says a variation exists.

On the Mac

The same rules, with room to spare. The window keeps the full move list beside the board, so the iPhone’s move strip isn’t needed. The toolbar holds the few actions used during a game, and everything else goes to the menu bar, where Mac users look for it.

Rename
Window title “Ruy Lopez.bchess”

The title shows the players and the subtitle the opening, as on iPhone. The file name stays in the title’s menu and the proxy icon, as in every document app.

Move
◀ ▶ buttons in the sidebar

They join the toolbar as one back/forward pair, with ← and → on the keyboard and ⌘← / ⌘→ for the start and end.

Move
Board ▾ toolbar menu

Becomes a Game menu in the menu bar with shortcuts. New Game and Flip Board also get toolbar buttons because they are used during play.

Move
Copy & Paste ▾ toolbar menu

Goes to the standard Edit menu: ⌘C copies the game, ⌥⌘C the position, ⌘V pastes either. The toolbar menu goes away.

Remove
Game name row (“-”)

Same as iPhone. The opening becomes the sidebar’s heading.

Simplify
Engine readout

The same sentence, evaluation, bar and best line as iPhone, at the bottom of the sidebar while Show Engine is on. Depth, nodes and speed only appear with “Show engine statistics” in Settings (decided, D3).

Keep
The full move list

The Mac has the room, so the sidebar keeps every move with variations in place, as a two-column list a chess player reads like a score sheet.

Decisions

Engine on iPhone: button or always on?

Decided A button, off by default. It keeps the screen calm and stops the engine from coaching during a game.

Analyze Game and Practice Openings on iPhone

Decided Left out for now. They stay in the Mac’s Game menu; the iPhone app gets simpler first.

Engine statistics on the Mac

Decided Behind “Show engine statistics” in Settings, off by default.

Board colors

Decided Unchanged in this pass.

New Game on the Mac

Today New Game restarts the open document in place, while File › New opens another window. On iPhone, New Game now adds a game and keeps the old one. Recommend: on the Mac, New Game (⌘N) opens a new window with the players sheet, so the finished game is kept, as on iPhone. Restarting in place goes away.
